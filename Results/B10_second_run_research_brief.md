# NVDA research brief

**Thesis:** Nvidia posted 65% YoY revenue growth to $215.9B and EPS of $4.90 on relentless AI/data-center demand, but gross margin compressed to 71.1% on a $4.5B China-related charge. The stock trades at a rich P/B (22.9x) with high beta (2.13) amid 10-year yields at 5.09% and sits below its 50-day SMA despite an intact longer-term uptrend and $150B buyback. Maintain cautious conviction; watch yields and China export developments. Not investment advice.

## Bull case
- Revenue grew 65% YoY to $215.94B, led by AI/data-center demand (Compute & Networking segment, per company 10-K segment disclosure, ~90% of total revenue).
- Strong profitability: 60.4% operating margin, 55.6% net margin, $96.7B free cash flow, minimal leverage (debt/equity 0.07x).
- Forward P/E of 13.6x is well below trailing P/E of 27.4x, implying consensus expects continued strong earnings growth.
- $150B buyback authorization and AI ecosystem expansion (Cisco, Adobe, European AI deals) signal confidence; a reported but unconfirmed potential $40B SpaceX chip purchase could add further demand if finalized.
- Analyst consensus remains 'strong buy' (59 analysts), and shares sit above the 200-day moving average, indicating an intact longer-term uptrend.

## Bear case
- Gross margin compressed from 75.0% to 71.1% on a $4.5B H20 inventory charge tied to China export restrictions, exposing geopolitical cost risk.
- International revenue's share of total fell to 31% from 41% YoY (dollar value not shown to have declined), and ongoing export controls could further disadvantage Nvidia versus less-restricted competitors.
- High valuation multiples (P/B 22.9x, ROE 117.2% on a thin equity base) leave little room for error if growth decelerates.
- Elevated beta (2.13) and 37.3% annualized volatility could amplify declines in a market selloff, compounded by the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.09% pressuring high-multiple growth stocks.
- Operational/reputational risks: claims of unsold chip inventory in warehouses, alleged China chip smuggling, RTX 5090 supply shortages, and pending legal disputes (Groq-related).

## Fundamentals
FY2026 revenue reached $215.94B (+65% YoY), with GAAP operating margin of 60.4%, net margin 55.6%, and diluted EPS of $4.90. Operating cash flow was $102.7B and free cash flow $96.7B; leverage remains minimal (debt/equity 0.07x, $11.04B debt vs. $157.3B equity). Per Nvidia's reported segment disclosures, Compute & Networking (data center/AI) revenue was approximately $193.5B (~90% of total, +67% YoY) versus Graphics (gaming) at roughly $22.5B (+57% YoY); these specific segment figures could not be independently cross-verified against raw underlying data, so treat them as approximate. Valuation is elevated on a P/B basis (22.9x), though forward P/E (13.6x) sits well below trailing P/E (27.4x), reflecting expected earnings growth.

## News and catalysts
News flow is net neutral (57 neutral vs. 22 positive vs. 16 negative). Positive catalysts include a $150B buyback expansion, a new AI-agent safety platform backed by 100+ organizations, and continued AI infrastructure demand including European deals, GMI Cloud financing, and a reported but unconfirmed potential $40B SpaceX chip purchase, alongside partnerships with Cisco and Adobe. Offsetting these: claims of unsold chip inventory sitting in warehouses, alleged chip smuggling into China, RTX 5090 supply shortages/production halts, a high-severity GPU monitoring vulnerability, and legal disputes including a former-Groq-engineer lawsuit tied to a reported $20B deal.

## Market and macro
NVDA closed at $217.09, down 2.33% over the past month but up 19.4% over the past year, with 37.28% annualized volatility and a beta of 2.13 versus SPY. The stock trades below its 50-day moving average ($222.94) but above its 200-day moving average ($203.12), suggesting near-term weakness within a longer-term uptrend. Macro conditions show CPI up 3.72% YoY, Fed funds rate easing to 3.75%, but the 10-year Treasury yield rising sharply to 5.09% from 3.99% a year ago — a headwind for high-multiple growth stocks — alongside real GDP growth of 2.19% YoY and unemployment at 4.2%.

## Conclusion
Nvidia's fundamentals remain strong, with 65% YoY revenue growth, robust margins, and substantial free cash flow generation. However, premium valuation, high beta, geopolitical/export-control exposure, and a rising 10-year yield warrant caution on timing and position sizing. Maintain a balanced, risk-aware stance rather than full conviction; a reversal of China-charge margin drag or a pullback in the 10-year yield would strengthen the bull case, while further export-control escalation or a sustained break below the 200-day SMA would argue for trimming exposure. This is analysis, not investment advice.
