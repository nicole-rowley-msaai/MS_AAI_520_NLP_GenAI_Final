# JPM research brief

**Thesis:** JPMorgan trades at 13.99x trailing P/E with FY2025 revenue of $181.85B and ROE 18.01%, backed by a 'buy' consensus and $372.57 target. Near-term headwinds include a Q3 earnings/revenue miss, rising criticized credit exposure, and a pullback below the 50-day SMA, though shares remain above the 200-day SMA. Macro crosscurrents—Fed cuts alongside a steepening 10-year yield—cloud the NIM outlook. Stance: constructive but not aggressive; monitor credit quality and margins.

## Bull case
- Profitability: ROE 18.01%, net margin 34.59%, valuation 13.99x trailing/13.08x forward P/E vs $372.57 target and 'buy' consensus (23 analysts).
- FY2025 revenue rose to $181.85B from $169.44B; diluted EPS rose to $20.02 from $19.75 despite a slight net income decline.
- Balance sheet scale: total assets grew to $4.42T, stockholders' equity $362.44B.
- Strategic/shareholder items: $20B Qatar sovereign wealth partnership ($15B public-equities mandate), dividend increase, $539B card spending with improving credit, raised income outlook.
- Shares trade above the 200-day SMA ($322.42), with a 1-year return of 11.72% and below-market beta of 0.87.

## Bear case
- Earnings miss: a Q3 EPS/revenue miss (described as rare) plus a reported $658M investment-securities loss raise near-term earnings-momentum questions.
- Credit quality softening: nonperforming assets rose to $4.807B from $4.038B; consumer/retail criticized exposure rose to $8.0B from $6.9B; office CRE is only 47% investment-grade with 80% drawn.
- Leverage rising: debt-to-equity up to 1.379x from 1.318x, alongside sharply negative operating cash flow (-$147.78B vs -$42.01B).
- Technical weakness: shares down 4.62% over the past month and trading below the 50-day SMA ($347.64).
- Macro pressure: 10-year yield rose to 5.09% from 3.99% despite Fed funds cuts to 3.75% from 4.22%, a steepening that could pressure NIM and bond valuations; Dimon has warned easy credit conditions may not persist.

## Fundamentals
FY2025 total revenue was $181.85B (net income $57.05B, diluted EPS $20.02) vs FY2024 revenue of $169.44B (net income $58.47B, diluted EPS $19.75); diluted EPS rose year-over-year despite a net income decline, a dynamic not further explained by available data. Total assets expanded to $4.42T from $4.00T, with stockholders' equity of $362.44B and debt-to-equity rising to 1.379x from 1.318x. Valuation remains undemanding at 13.99x trailing / 13.08x forward P/E and 2.52x price/book, with ROE of 18.01% and net margin of 34.59%. Credit metrics show softening: nonperforming assets rose to $4.807B from $4.038B and consumer/retail criticized exposure rose to $8.0B from $6.9B, while office CRE exposure (47% investment-grade, 80% drawn) is a notable risk concentration.

## News and catalysts
News sentiment is broadly neutral (37 neutral vs. 24 positive, 14 negative). Positive catalysts: a $20B Qatar sovereign wealth fund partnership with a $15B public-equities mandate, a dividend increase, $539B in card spending with improving credit trends, and a raised income outlook. Offsetting these are a Q3 earnings and revenue miss, a reported $658M investment-securities loss, employment-related litigation, and management commentary on AI-related operational risks and safeguards. Dimon has also cautioned that easy credit conditions may not persist, which could pressure corporate credit markets.

## Market and macro
JPM closed at $333.53, up 11.72% over the past year but down 4.62% over the past month, with 22.12% annualized volatility and a beta of 0.87 vs SPY. Shares sit below the 50-day SMA ($347.64) but above the 200-day SMA ($322.42), indicating a near-term pullback within a longer-term uptrend. Macro backdrop: CPI rose to 336.298 from 324.245, the Fed funds rate eased to 3.75% from 4.22%, yet the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.09% from 3.99%—a steepening long end despite cuts. Unemployment improved to 4.2% from 4.4%, and real GDP grew to $24,408.0B from $23,884.6B.

## Conclusion
JPMorgan's profitability, revenue growth, and reasonable valuation relative to a 'buy' consensus $372.57 target support a constructive long-term view, tempered by a Q3 earnings miss, rising leverage, softening credit in consumer and office CRE segments, and near-term technical weakness. A steepening 10-year yield despite Fed cuts adds uncertainty around net interest margin trajectory. JPM appears reasonably positioned but warrants monitoring of credit quality, leverage, and margin trends before adding exposure. Analysis only; not investment advice.
